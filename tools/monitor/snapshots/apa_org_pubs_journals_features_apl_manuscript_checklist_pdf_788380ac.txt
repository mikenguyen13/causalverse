Manuscript Preparation Instructions
In an effort to enhance transparency, reproducibility, and replicability, we require authors to use the
following checklists in preparing submissions to the Journal of Applied Psychology. There are five
sections below for: (1) primary quantitative studies, (2) meta-analytic research, (3) qualitative and mixed-
methods studies and (4) studies using machine learning, (5) simulation and computational modeling
studies. Authors should use these instructions as a guide, as this is not a comprehensive list. It is also the
authors’ responsibility to check text against tables for consistent and accurate statistical reporting
(manually or using tools like statcheck.io). Also note that not all checklist items will apply to every
manuscript. We also encourage authors to consult with the APA Journal Article Reporting Standards
(JARS; https://apastyle.apa.org/jars).
Primary Quantitative Studies
Method
____ Sampling plan clearly described, including recruitment strategy and power analysis (as
appropriate), any inclusion/exclusion criteria (including outliers detected and removed or winsorized; e.g.,
adults working more than 40 hours a week), and number of cases excluded.
____ For data collected via online, panel-based, or crowd-sourced platforms, provide detailed
information about the data collection procedures. This includes basic information such as the name of the
platform and the compensation offered per participant. This also includes the techniques used to prevent
and detect non-human responses (e.g., bot-generated responses, AI-assisted responses, etc.) as well as
problematic human responding (e.g., careless responding due to inattention or fatigue; fraudulent
responding due to misrepresentation of identity, eligibility, or location, etc.). Specify the relevant design-
level protections (i.e., features built into the survey), implementation-level checks (i.e., procedures used
during data collection to detect and manage such responses), and post hoc screening/exclusion
procedures. Report the number and percentage of respondents flagged and excluded at each stage and
describe any sensitivity analyses conducted to evaluate how inclusion or exclusion of flagged
responses/respondents affects the substantive results. For each procedure, clarify whether it is
automatically implemented by the platform, manually implemented by the authors, or a combination of
both.
____ Final sample size associated with each analysis included in text and/or tables, along with
response rate in text. This includes differences between final sample and excluded data, or a change in
sample size as a result of data matching. Effective sample sizes might be reported as a function of
missing data analyses (e.g., multiple imputation).
____ Amount and treatment of missing data reported at the scale and the item levels (e.g., listwise
deletion, pairwise deletion, multiple imputation, full information maximum likelihood [FIML] estimation).
____ For experimental research, provide sample size per condition; for within-subjects designs, report
correlations between conditions. For multi-level/cross-level research, report total sample size at each
level of analysis, along with descriptive statistics at each level (e.g., means, SDs, and correlations).
____ Report full and observed scale range, along with scale anchors, and number of items for all
measures.
____ For adapted or shortened measures, rationales and specific modifications are described. If using
measures originally developed in Study A and then adapted in Study B, acknowledge both studies
appropriately.
____ For translated scales/measures, translation-back translation described (e.g., number and
qualifications of translators, item modifications and/or deletions, how disagreements were resolved).
Note: Simply stating that Brislin (1970, 1984, 1986) recommendations were followed is not sufficient.
____ Experimental manipulations, research protocols, and materials fully described in the manuscript.
For measures and research materials, either (1) include sample content (e.g., 2–3 sample items and
response scale) along with the citation/website/repository where the full content can be obtained or (2)
provide the full content if you have obtained copyright permission from the original publisher. Proprietary
measures or materials should be noted as such and require citation but not sample items. Please be
aware that photographs of individuals may require copyright permission from the original publisher.
____ For longitudinal designs, time-lagged design, and experience sampling methodology, provide
rationale for measurement time interval(s) and number of measurement occasions.
____ For path analysis, factor analysis, and structural equation models, include
information on the specification of the measurement model (e.g., individual items vs. parcels, linear vs.
ordinal, identification constraints, any correlated error residuals) and degrees of freedom checked for
accuracy (see https://gmuiopsych.shinyapps.io/degreesoffreedom/).
Inclusive Reporting
____ Report year and location that data were collected and describe efforts to obtain a sample
representative of the broader population of interest.
____ Ensure that race and ethnicity are not used interchangeably.
____ If available, report the percentage of people in each unique racial and/or ethnic group rather than
reporting “percent non-White”. If data on race/ethnicity was not collected, provide rationale.
____ Use the group that is central to research question(s) as the reference group (i.e., do not default to
using White participants as the referent).
____ Provide an interpretation of race and/or ethnicity within the context/nation/country in which
research was conducted.
____ Report sociodemographic information (e.g., age, sex, race/ethnicity, education level, job tenure,
relevant occupation information, organizational tenure, years of working experience) and research context
information (e.g., country, industry, occupation, job, work tasks).
Analyses/Results
____ Manipulation checks reported for experimental studies, including how failed manipulations were
handled. Relevant analysis for each manipulation check presented for multiple predictor designs.
____ If AI or Large Language Model (LLM) was used to generate analytic codes, disclose and include
the prompts and codes used in appendix or supplemental materials.
____ Means, standard deviations, correlations, and reliability coefficients (if applicable) reported for all
measured variables, including sociodemographic characteristics, control variables, and transformed/non-
linear variables (e.g., interactive and curvilinear terms). When appropriate, skewness and kurtosis for
relevant variables are reported.
____ Full results from model testing reported (e.g., if testing 3-way interaction, report its ΔR2 over the
all possible 2-way interactions and the main effects entered into the model).
____ If testing moderated mediation/mediated moderation, include all relevant path coefficients
necessary for estimating the statistical model (e.g., the direct effect of the predictor on the outcome, the
direct effect of the moderator on the mediator/outcome), as well as indirect and conditional indirect
effects; if unstandardized regression estimates are reported, also include intercepts/constants and
standard errors. If applicable, the index of moderated mediation should be reported.
____ Appropriate effect size estimates included (e.g., r, R2, ΔR2, R, ΔR, Cohen’s f2, Cohen’s d, η2,
odds ratio). Confidence and/or credibility intervals around effect sizes are preferred.
____ For each hypothesis testing, when possible, include the observed power for the specific test,
which can help gauge the replicability of the finding (for z-test or t-test based hypothesis testing, you can
directly calculate the observed power using a ShinyApp located here:
https://japplied.shinyapps.io/observedpower/).
____ Information provided in tables and/or text describing how dichotomous/categorical variables
(including control variables) were coded. The direction of coding should be clear (e.g., 0 = men, 1 =
women).
____ Standard errors, confidence and/or credibility intervals, and/or test statistics (e.g., t-value) with p-
value reported. Alpha level and/or confidence interval specified. State whether any correction procedure
is applied when testing a large number of hypotheses.
____ For mono-level path model analysis, provide rationale for preferring this approach over structural
equation modeling with latent variable specifications. If possible, report supplemental analysis using
structural equation modeling to assess the robustness of the findings.
____ When reporting SEM model statistics, include at minimum: the chi-square test (χ²) with degrees of
freedom and p-value; fit indices (e.g., CFI); misfit/error indices with confidence intervals (e.g., RMSEA);
and discrepancy indices (e.g., SRMR).
____ For multilevel data involving teams or other collective entities, aggregation models (e.g., direct
consensus, referent-shift, configural), within-group agreement (e.g., rWG(j)), and centering decisions
(e.g., uncentered, group-mean centering, grand-mean centering, latent centering) are justified and
reported. If different centering approaches are applied to different variables, specify and explain the
rationale for each choice.
____ For multilevel models, model testing procedures and relevant statistics reported (e.g., ICCs,
variance component estimates).
____ For plots of moderation on a Likert-scale based continuous variable (e.g., slopes at +/–1 SD, or
Johnson-Neyman intervals), results reported should use relative (“higher” vs. “lower”) not absolute (“high”
vs. “low”) language, and raw values should be reported for the levels selected. Plots should be scaled in a
manner that represents effects appropriately. The levels of the moderator used in simple slope analyses
should be within the actual range of observed data.
____ Continuous data (e.g., a moderator variable) should not be converted to binary or categorical data
without sufficient rationale.
____ Estimation procedure and methods reported for more advanced analytical approaches (e.g., dfs
or df correction, bootstrapping approach, Bayesian credibility interval).
_____ For any Bayesian analysis effect sizes (e.g., credibility intervals, HDI, Bayes factors) or any
Bayesian estimation of mode statistics (e.g., Bayesian SEM), report each prior distribution used, specify
and explain the rationale for each choice.
____ Statistical packages and programs (including version number) used for data analysis cited.
____ Alternative data analysis (e.g., including a variable that was subsequently dropped through the
review process or during the original analysis) and robustness tests described in footnote or appendix.
____ Text checked against tables for consistent and accurate statistical reporting.
Meta-Analytic Research
____ PRISMA-type search flowchart included to describe search protocols (http://www.prisma-
statement.org/). At a minimum, the flowchart should include all keywords and Boolean operators (i.e., full
search strings), the specific databases and other data sources reported, and the specific number of
search results by each database/data source.
____ Information provided about inter-coder agreement/reliability, how the agreement is calculated,
coder qualifications/calibrations/training (if necessarily, anonymized for review), and methods used to
resolve inter-coder discrepancies.
____ If corrections for psychometric artifacts are applied (e.g., unreliability, range restriction), then the
method used (e.g., individual correction, artifact-distribution, Case II, Case IV, Case V) should be
reported in text with rationale, as well as artifact distribution statistics (e.g., mean and SD of the relevant
artifact, how missing reliability estimates were handled) and the order in which corrections were applied.
____ Justification provided for corrections/no corrections for psychometric artifacts. Bare-bones meta-
analytical results should be provided along with fully corrected effect sizes.
____ Information on each primary study, relevant code(s), reliability and moderator information (where
relevant), sample size(s), and effect size(s) included in appendix or made available online (e.g., Open
Science Framework, osf.io).
____ If the Schmidt-Hunter method is used, all relevant results are reported by referring to
recommended result tables. Tables should include: number of independent samples; total sample size;
sample size weighted mean observed effect size; sample size weighted standard deviation of observed
effect size, mean true score effect size true score standard deviation; credibility interval; confidence
interval around the mean true effect, and percentage of variance attributable to statistical artifacts.
____ Confidence and/or credibility intervals used when interpreting meta-analytic estimates rather than
NHST.
____ For moderator testing, justify choice of method (e.g., subgroup, meta-regression). For the
subgroup approach, describe the rationale for moderator testing. Provide justification if continuous
moderators are dichotomized for subgroup analysis.
____ For meta-regression, report and justify the type of model (e.g., mixed versus fixed effects model)
and the estimator (e.g., restricted maximum likelihood estimator) used. When the number of studies is
small, report the pertinent method (e.g., Knapp & Hartung, 2003) to mitigate Type I error. If more than one
effect size is available for a cell in the constructed meta-analytical correlation matrix, provide justification
for the chosen effect size.
____ Publication bias check (including results) reported in text and/or tables or made available online
(e.g., osf.io).
Qualitative and Mixed-Methods Research
____ Qualitative approach (e.g., grounded theory, phenomenological, thematic analysis, consensual
qualitative research) described.
____ Data collection procedures (e.g., context of data collection, participant recruitment, interview
protocol, observational methods, time in field, number of researchers involved in data collection)
described.
____ Level or unit of analysis (e.g., individual word, meaningful thought, participant) specified.
____ If coding taxonomy was used, development of codes and coding procedures is described in
detail. Total number of coders, number of coders per code, coder training/expertise, and coder agreement
(e.g., percent agreement or hit rate, Cohen’s or Fleiss’ kappa) reported.
____ If relevant, statistical packages and programs (including version number) used for data analysis
are reported.
____ Clearly describe how raw data were analyzed and/or interpreted. Direct quotations,
observations or other examples of raw data included.
____ Consult with the APA JARS Guidelines (https://apastyle.apa.org/jars/qualitative and
https://apastyle.apa.org/jars/mixed-methods) as appropriate.
____ For additional information, see Levitt, H. M., Bamberg, M., Creswell, J. W., Frost, D. M.,
Joseelong, R., & Suarez-Orozco, C. (2018). Journal reporting standards for qualitative primary, qualitative
meta-analytic, and mixed methods research in psychology: The APA Publications and Communications
Board task force report. American Psychologist, 73, 26-46. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/amp0000151
Machine Learning
____ Describe and data preprocessing or data transformations (feature engineering) performed, both
for quantitative data (e.g., converting non-numeric to numeric, encoding categorical data, data
aggregation) and text data (e.g., stemming, lemmatization, n-grams, tokenization).
____ Describe the process used to deal with any missing data or outliers and how those outliers were
identified.
____ Provide information regarding the distribution of all variables.
____ In the case of classification data, if class imbalance is present, describe the methods used to
address this (e.g., over- and undersampling techniques).
____ Describe the analysis in transparent details (e.g., what is the nature of the algorithm, how was the
chosen model selected, describe the underlying parameters of the model, explain the cross-validation
and model-tuning process).
____ For neural network architecture, describe the various layers (e.g., nature of the embedding layer,
number of hidden layers, number of nodes per layer).
____ Describe the process for model tuning: what parameters were tuned, what range of values were
evaluated, and how those values were selected (e.g., random grid search, what criterion was optimized,
etc.).
____ Describe the process for model validation, including both internal and external model validation.
Transparently report cross-validation techniques (e.g., k-fold cross-validation, nested-cross validation).
____ When reporting validation metrics, report multiple metrics whenever appropriate
Whenever possible, report measures of effect size and confidence and/or credibility intervals along with
parameter estimates.
____ If using an LLM, detail the model (e.g., name, version number), and prompts used, the process
for prompt generation (e.g., hyperparameter settings, API vs. web client), and evaluation.
____ Cite the software, programs, and packages used and provide the code when possible.
Simulation and Computational Modeling
____ Specify the primary purpose of the model (e.g., theory building, theory testing, integrating
literatures, exploring boundary conditions, evaluating interventions, or methodological demonstration).
____ Conduct and report sensitivity or robustness analyses (e.g., varying key parameters, initial
conditions, or structural assumptions) and describe how conclusions do or do not change.
____ Ensure that simulations and models capture and accurately reflect all hypotheses being tested.
____ Provide a clear description of the model structure and processes (e.g., entities, state variables,
environment, decision rules, update schedule) in enough detail that an informed reader could
reimplement the model.
____ Report and justify the range of parameters being simulated (both fixed and free).
____ Compare more complex simulations and models against simpler versions, when appropriate.
Ensure that ‘winning’ models undergo stringent tests that guard against model overfitting.
____ Rerun simulations and models, when appropriate, reporting the random number generator seed
values used, to help ensure that global optima were reached (vs. trapped in local minima).
____ Provide useful information the extends beyond the average effects for each condition (e.g., SDs
for each condition, d-values between conditions, visualizations of variability across simulation or model
runs)
____ Report the implementation platform, software, and versions (e.g., NetLogo, Mesa in Python, R
with specific packages).
____ Share all simulation or model code (and data, if they cannot be generated easily on one’s own).
